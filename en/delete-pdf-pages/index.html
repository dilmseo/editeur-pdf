    What the conversion keeps, and what it loses

    Working inside a browser comes with limits a hosted service does not have. Here they are in plain words, so you know what to expect before you start.

    What is kept

    • The exact quality of the pages you keep
    • Selectable text

    What is not carried over

    • Filled form fields
    • Electronic signatures

    Worth knowing about the format

    Unlike redacting in the editor, which covers things visually, deletion genuinely removes the page from the document: its content can no longer be recovered from the file you get.

    Why it's free

    Online converters receive your file on their servers, process it, then promise to delete it. Every document costs them computing time: that is why they charge, and why they cap file sizes.

    Here, the work happens on your own computer. The page contains no server code: your document cannot be received, because it is never sent. So there is nothing to bill, nothing to cap, and nothing to delete afterwards. Cut your connection once the page has loaded — everything keeps working.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is the deleted content really gone?

    Yes. The page is not copied into the document produced: its text and images are simply not there any more. That is the difference from the editor's redaction, which only covers.

    Can I delete everything?

    No: a PDF must keep at least one page. The tool stops you if your selection would leave nothing.

    Is the rest of the document changed?

    No, the pages you keep are copied identically: no recompression, no change of size.
