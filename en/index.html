The paywall is editing the text

Online editors happily let you add text on top of the document. But fixing a line that is already written — the very thing you came for — almost always requires a subscription. And your file left for their servers before you even noticed.

The usual services

  • Editing existing text: paid
  • Scanned document recognition: paid too
  • Your file leaves for a remote server
  • Size capped, often at 100 MB
  • Account required, or a watermark on the result

Here

  • Editing existing text: free
  • Scan recognition included, no extra charge
  • The file never leaves your browser
  • No size or count limit
  • No account, no watermark; source code public

Why it's free

Online converters receive your file on their servers, process it, then promise to delete it. Every document costs them computing time: that is why they charge, and why they cap file sizes.

Here, the work happens on your own computer. The page contains no server code: your document cannot be received, because it is never sent. So there is nothing to bill, nothing to cap, and nothing to delete afterwards. Cut your connection once the page has loaded — everything keeps working.

The questions we get

What does it cost?

Nothing, and there is nothing to buy: no paid tier, no limited trial, no watermark. That includes fixing existing text and reading scanned documents, which online services almost all keep for their paid plans. The reason is simple: for them every document burns server time. Here your own computer does the work, so there is nothing to bill.

Is my file really private?

Yes. The PDF is opened and modified by your browser; no data is sent anywhere. The site contains no server code at all: it could not receive your file even if it wanted to. You can check by cutting your connection once the page has loaded — everything keeps working.

Is hidden text really erased?

No, and that matters: replaced text is covered visually but stays inside the file — a copy and paste can bring it back. To truly remove sensitive data, redact the area then reprint the result as a PDF (Print → Save as PDF): the old text is then gone for good.

Is the original font kept?

The text is rewritten in a close standard font — Helvetica, Times or Courier — picked automatically from the document, with bold detected by comparing the measured width against the font metrics. On a common typeface the result is very close; on a distinctive one the difference shows. The right-hand panel lets you correct it by hand.

Is there a size or page limit?

None imposed. The only constraint is your computer memory, and pages are rendered as you scroll so a thick document stays smooth.

Can I use it offline?

Yes. Once the page has loaded you can cut your internet connection. The code can also be downloaded from the public repository for fully local use, including the small development server needed for character recognition.
