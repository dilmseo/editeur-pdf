    What the conversion keeps, and what it loses

    Working inside a browser comes with limits a hosted service does not have. Here they are in plain words, so you know what to expect before you start.

    What is kept

    • Everything inside the frame, with no loss
    • Selectable text
    • Image resolution
    • The file weight, almost unchanged

    What is not carried over

    • Whatever falls outside the frame is no longer displayed
    • The same framing applies to every page

    Worth knowing about the format

    Cropping changes the displayed area of the page, not its content. Technically, what falls outside stays in the file but is no longer shown: to truly erase it, reprint the result as a PDF afterwards.

    Why it's free

    Online converters receive your file on their servers, process it, then promise to delete it. Every document costs them computing time: that is why they charge, and why they cap file sizes.

    Here, the work happens on your own computer. The page contains no server code: your document cannot be received, because it is never sent. So there is nothing to bill, nothing to cap, and nothing to delete afterwards. Cut your connection once the page has loaded — everything keeps working.

    Frequently asked questions

    Does the framing apply to every page?

    Yes, the same frame is applied throughout. That is the dominant case, a scan having the same margins from first page to last.

    Is the cropped content removed from the file?

    No: it is merely hidden by the new display area. If the point is to erase information, reprint the result as a PDF after cropping.

    Does the file get lighter?

    Barely: nothing is re-encoded. To genuinely lighten it, follow up with compression.
