    What the conversion keeps, and what it loses

    Working inside a browser comes with limits a hosted service does not have. Here they are in plain words, so you know what to expect before you start.

    What is kept

    • Every image at its original size and resolution
    • Total sharpness: no lossy compression
    • The order in which you add the files
    • Flat colours, with no bleeding

    What is not carried over

    • Transparency, replaced by the page background
    • Nothing else: the image is embedded as it is

    Need the other direction? PDF → PNG does exactly the opposite, with the same guarantees.

    Worth knowing about the format

    PNG is embedded without recompression: a screenshot stays readable letter by letter, where a trip through JPG would leave a halo around the characters.

    Why it's free

    Online converters receive your file on their servers, process it, then promise to delete it. Every document costs them computing time: that is why they charge, and why they cap file sizes.

    Here, the work happens on your own computer. The page contains no server code: your document cannot be received, because it is never sent. So there is nothing to bill, nothing to cap, and nothing to delete afterwards. Cut your connection once the page has loaded — everything keeps working.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is the transparency of my PNGs kept?

    A PDF page has no transparent background: translucent areas land on the white of the page. If transparency matters, flatten the image onto the background you want before converting.

    How many images can I gather?

    As many as you like: no limit is imposed. The PDF simply weighs the sum of the images.

    Do screenshots stay sharp?

    Perfectly: the PNG is embedded without recompression, so the text of a capture stays as legible as in the original image.
